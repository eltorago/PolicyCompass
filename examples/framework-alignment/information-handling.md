# Information classification and handling policy

Fictional document for the PolicyCompass demonstration. Version 1.0.
Owner: Information Manager. Approver: Chief Executive. Review: every 12 months.

## Purpose and scope

This policy explains how staff classify, store, share and dispose of business
information. It applies to electronic records, printed material, portable devices
and information held by suppliers. The organisation uses four internal handling
labels: Public, Internal, Sensitive and Restricted. These labels do not represent
a government security clearance or an authority to release another party's records.

## Ownership and classification

Each information collection has a business owner recorded in the information
asset register. The owner assigns its classification based on the harm that
unauthorised disclosure, alteration or loss could cause. Personal information,
commercial negotiations and security credentials receive additional access
restrictions. Owners review classifications annually and when the purpose or
sensitivity of a collection changes.

Staff label documents and shared folders using the approved handling labels.
Information received from another party keeps any agreed handling restrictions.
An owner must approve a lower classification or public release and record the
reason. Where mixed information is combined, the most restrictive handling rules
apply until the owner approves a separated version.

## Storage and access

Staff store business records in approved repositories with access restricted to
authorised roles. Sensitive and Restricted information is encrypted at rest and
in transit using the organisation's approved services. The technology team manages
encryption keys separately from protected data and limits key administration to
designated staff. Credentials are held in the approved secrets store.

Portable devices use disk encryption and automatic screen locking. Staff keep
paper records in locked storage when unattended and collect printed sensitive
documents promptly. Visitors are escorted in work areas containing restricted
records. Personal devices and removable media require specific approval before
they can hold sensitive business information.

## Sharing and records

Before sharing, staff check the recipient's identity, purpose and authority to
receive the information. Sensitive transfers use an approved secure channel and
the minimum information needed for the task. Links to shared files have named
recipients and an expiry date where the service supports it. A supplier agreement
must describe permitted use, access, storage location and return or deletion.

The Information Manager maintains retention schedules and records any legal hold
that suspends disposal. Business owners keep records of approvals, decisions and
transactions in the designated records system. Staff report misdirected messages,
lost records and unexpected access as incidents immediately.

## Disposal and review

Records are disposed of only after the owner confirms that the retention period
has ended and no hold applies. The technology team sanitises electronic storage
media before reuse or disposal and verifies the outcome. Paper records requiring
protection are securely destroyed. Disposal records identify the material,
authorisation, method and date; suppliers provide destruction evidence where used.

Exceptions need the Information Manager's written approval, a risk assessment and
an expiry date. The owner reviews this policy annually and after a significant
information loss or change to the organisation's records obligations.
