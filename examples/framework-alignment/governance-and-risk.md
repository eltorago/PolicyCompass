# Cyber security governance and risk policy

Fictional document for the PolicyCompass demonstration. Version 1.0.
Owner: Risk Manager. Approver: Governing Board. Review: every 12 months.

## Purpose and scope

The organisation protects the information and services that people rely on to do
their work. This policy assigns responsibility for cyber security decisions and
sets out how risks are assessed, funded and reviewed. It covers business units,
shared services, projects and outsourced activities.

## Accountability

The governing board approves the cyber security strategy and risk appetite. The
Chief Executive assigns an executive sponsor and funds the agreed security
programme. The Head of Technology manages technical safeguards; the Risk Manager
maintains the risk assessment method and challenges overdue treatment actions.
Business service owners remain accountable for risks affecting their services,
including services delivered by suppliers.

The security steering committee meets quarterly to review incidents, material
risks, exceptions, assurance findings and progress against the security plan.
It reports decisions and unresolved matters to the executive team. Staff report
suspected incidents and weaknesses through the service desk and cooperate with
reviews relevant to their work.

## Assess and treat risk

Service owners assess cyber risks before introducing a new service, sharing
sensitive information with a supplier or making a major system change. Reviews
consider threats, vulnerabilities, existing safeguards, likelihood and the
consequences for service delivery, people and information. Owners record the
assessment in the risk register and review it at least annually.

Every risk treatment plan identifies the action, accountable owner, funding,
target date and evidence needed to close it. Service owners propose whether to
treat, avoid, transfer or accept a risk. Risks above the approved tolerance are
referred to the executive team for a decision. Acceptance must state the remaining
risk, reasons, review date and approving authority.

## Policy and planning

The security team maintains a policy register with an owner, approval date,
review date and version for each policy. Staff can access the current policies
from the internal policy library. Managers explain changes affecting their teams
and remove obsolete copies from local guidance.

The annual security plan links priority risks to funded improvements. Project
teams involve security, privacy and records staff early enough to change a design
before committing to a purchase. The Risk Manager maintains a register of legal,
regulatory and contractual obligations and refers uncertain obligations for advice.

## Exceptions and assurance

An exception request states the requirement affected, reason, risk owner,
compensating safeguards and expiry date. The Head of Technology reviews technical
impacts and the Risk Manager checks the assessment before the accountable
executive decides. Exceptions are recorded centrally and reviewed monthly;
expired exceptions require a new decision.

Independent reviews test selected controls against evidence of their operation.
The committee receives measures for unresolved vulnerabilities, overdue access
reviews, training completion, recovery exercises and incident follow-up actions.
The policy owner reviews this policy every year and after a material change to
services, obligations or the organisation's risk position.
