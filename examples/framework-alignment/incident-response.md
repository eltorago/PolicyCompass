# Cyber incident response and monitoring policy

Fictional document for the PolicyCompass demonstration. Version 1.0.
Owner: Security Manager. Approver: Chief Executive. Review: every 12 months.

## Purpose and scope

This policy explains how the organisation detects, reports and responds to cyber
incidents. It covers suspected compromise of accounts, devices, business services
and information, including services operated by suppliers. Staff can report a
suspicion without first proving that a breach occurred.

## Detection and reporting

Staff report suspected cyber incidents immediately to the service desk using the
published contact channel. The on-call contact is available outside business
hours for urgent reports. Staff preserve relevant messages and follow containment
instructions; they must not investigate another person's account themselves.

The technology team collects security event logs from critical systems in a
central monitoring service. Logs include authentication events, changes to access
permissions and administrative actions. Access to logs is restricted, and logs
are protected against alteration and premature deletion. Retention periods follow
the approved logging schedule and the needs of an active investigation.

Analysts review alerts, correlate events from different systems and record the
reason for closing or escalating a case. Monitoring rules are reviewed after
incidents and when business systems change. Monitoring access is used only for
authorised security and service investigations.

## Triage and authority

The duty analyst records the report, affected assets, discovery time and known
impact in the incident register. Incidents are classified by likely harm to
people, information and critical services. The Security Manager appoints an
incident commander for serious cases and brings together technology, business,
privacy, legal and communications representatives.

The incident response plan assigns response roles, escalation paths and decision
authority. The incident commander can authorise isolation of affected devices,
revocation of access and suspension of a compromised service. Actions affecting
safety or essential service delivery are agreed with the responsible service owner.

## Containment, evidence and communication

Responders contain the incident, preserve evidence and establish its scope before
removing the cause. An evidence log records who collected each item, when it was
collected, where it is stored and every transfer of custody. Copies used for
investigation are protected from alteration. Responders keep a timeline of facts,
decisions, actions and remaining uncertainties.

The privacy and legal leads assess reporting obligations and record decisions
about regulator, customer and individual notifications. Communications staff
coordinate approved messages and update times. Suppliers must provide relevant
incident details and cooperate with containment and recovery under their contracts.

## Recovery and improvement

The service owner authorises restoration after the incident commander confirms
that containment and security checks are complete. Recovery follows the business
continuity and recovery policy. The Security Manager holds a review after every
serious incident and assigns improvement actions with owners and due dates.

The incident response plan is exercised annually with business teams and key
suppliers. The exercise tests escalation, evidence handling and communication as
well as technical response. Contact lists are checked quarterly. The policy and
response plans are updated after exercises, incidents and major service changes.
